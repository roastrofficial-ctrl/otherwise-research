# Generation 0.7 executive examination

**Result:** the selected research harness makes both domains indispensable under securely independent creation. Complete compromise of either domain alone no longer supplies a complete decryption capability. Compromise of sufficient members in **both** domains still permits early disclosure. Malicious provisioning or a shared administrator can collapse the separation. This is an unreviewed synthetic experiment, not production security.

**Q1. Can a fully malicious custody quorum disclose without the independent authorisation capability?** Under the explicit secure-creation/primitive/recipient/evidence assumptions, no: A recovers the real custody component with all extracted local secrets, bypasses all APIs, but cannot obtain B or a valid DEK. The missing component is mathematically necessary; it is not an approval flag. Finite failures are supported by the threshold-DH/AEAD assumptions and XOR access structure, not an exhaustive attack proof.

**Q2. Can a fully malicious authorisation quorum disclose without custody capability?** Symmetrically no under those assumptions; B recovers only its component. Enough custody secrets must remain unavailable, and honest indispensable holders must follow valid evidence. One honest holder alone is insufficient if the other two have already colluded.

**Q3. What if sufficient participants in both domains collude?** C decrypts before valid authorisation with raw point operations and both wrapped components. No reusable scalar reconstruction is required. This remains a successful attack, not a regression hidden by honest API checks.

**Q4. How is each contribution authenticated?** An owner-signed immutable sidecar commits membership, generation and recipient scope. Each holder signs exact v7 context/index/recipient-encrypted proof data with its pinned Ed25519 identity. TLS protects transport; it is not the only provenance check.

**Q5. How is arithmetic verified?** Recipient checks the inner/outer identity, committed verification share and Chaum–Pedersen DLEQ equations. A signed bad proof is rejected independently, leaving other valid responders usable. The Node/Python public proof corpus has 23 signed objects (6 valid, 17 negative) plus strict-parser negatives. This is not an independent full production crypto audit.

**Q6. How is collective release independently verified?** The separate recipient client has no owner signing seed. It starts from an independently authenticated expected sidecar/ciphertext and recipient key, fetches fresh pinned witness evidence, verifies the policy certificate, each contribution, distinct 2+2 thresholds, ciphertext AEAD and salted plaintext commitment. T executes that separate CLI; a SaaS success object supplies none of these facts.

**Q7. What stops cross-context replay?** Exact signed/proved context binds protocol/suite, Capsule, immutable manifest and sidecar, ciphertext, recipient, policy version, release attempt, finality certificate/authorisation, committee identities and key/Capsule epochs. G tests substitutions and duplicates. Identical authorised retries are allowed idempotently; new keys do not revoke already copied old secrets/ciphertext.

**Q8. Why does one malformed or unavailable participant no longer poison a healthy quorum?** Bounded concurrent collection validates responses individually and selects two compatible identities in each domain; no one-domain surplus or arrival-order vote substitutes for another requirement. Oversized, malformed, signed-invalid, duplicated, delayed and missing replies are excluded/audited. Witness reads are parallel and require3/4. Completion assumes sufficient valid responses and CPU/storage within the documented per-stage budgets; no global DoS or all-Byzantine-schedule availability proof is claimed.

**Q9. What exactly happens when disarm and release race?** Honest witnesses serialize first durable local FINAL/DISARM; conflicting three-signature certificates intersect honestly for f<=1. H tests early completed disarm blocking honest release and valid finality making late disarm unavailable. **V exposes an earlier limitation:** a local FINAL for an earlier PRESENT window can block disarm before the overall release policy is satisfied. Thus this generation does not promise cancellation until the last partial or complete policy decision. Already emitted sufficient capabilities and combined collusion cannot be undone.

**Q10. What dependencies were added and what if they fail?** A second independently administered 2/3 committee, its key backups/software/governance, recipient provisioning, parallel timing budgets and 3/4 encrypted-operation journals. Losing one holder per domain is tolerated; losing threshold blocks availability without inventing a recovery secret. Journals plus old encrypted backups recover later operations after one custody rollback/device loss. Actual witness process loss/restart is tested. Full committee replacement, proactive refresh, journal replica repair and safe infrastructure migration remain incomplete. Neither Laravel nor PorterNet is required.

**Q11. Which guarantees come from where?** XOR's two-component access structure and threshold group algebra supply mathematical necessity under the cryptographic assumptions. Legitimate timing/absence/finality and refusal to release the indispensable component rely on honest participants and authenticated complete-enough observation. Independent administrators, secure creation/recipient code, secret backup handling, trusted updates, durable clocks/storage and long-term maintenance are operational assumptions. Distinct keys/containers do not prove them; the integrated crypto has not been externally reviewed.

**Q12. Which successful attacks remain?** Combined-domain collusion (C), retained creator DEK/setup secrets (I), historical exposure surviving fresh rotation (J), shared administration (K), correlated dealer keys despite distinct public values (U), and early-slot finalisation denying cancellation (V). Historical static/0.6 custody-quorum attacks, compromised trusted clients, censorship causing real missed authoritative presence and erased/global anchors also remain. Their earlier implementations and transcripts are preserved and regressed.
